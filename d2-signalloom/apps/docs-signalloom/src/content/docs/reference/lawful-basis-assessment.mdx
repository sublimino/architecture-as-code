## 1. Why this Assessment Exists

Monitoring autonomous coding agents running on developer laptops touches employee personal data (process trees, timestamps, user identifiers, network traffic, and local file access patterns). Under European data protection law (EU/UK GDPR):

1. **Employee Monitoring Classification**: Regardless of whether telemetry is collected for a cybersecurity or platform engineering purpose, monitoring employee device activity constitutes personal data processing under GDPR Article 4(1).
2. **Consent is Invalid**: In an employment relationship, consent (Article 6(1)(a)) is legally invalid due to the inherent power imbalance between employer and employee (EDPB Guidelines 05/2020).
3. **Article 6(1)(f) is the Sole Applicable Basis**: Processing must be justified under **Legitimate Interests** (Article 6(1)(f)), requiring a rigorous three-limb test (Purpose, Necessity, Balancing).
4. **Recital 49 is Narrow**: While Recital 49 recognizes processing strictly necessary for network and information security, it does not provide an unconditional blanket exemption for broad workstation telemetry.

## 2. The Three-Limb Legitimate Interests Assessment (LIA)

### 2.1 Limb 1: The Purpose Test — **PASSED**
- **Controller Interest**: The organization has a legitimate interest in maintaining an accurate inventory of autonomous AI agents executing inside its boundary, ensuring corporate code is not sent to unapproved model endpoints, and meeting regulatory supply-chain obligations (e.g. EU AI Act, DORA Art. 11, NIS2).
- **Assessment**: The security attribution purpose is legitimate and clearly articulated.

### 2.2 Limb 2: The Necessity Test — **NOT PASSED (OPEN)**
To satisfy necessity, the controller must demonstrate that no less intrusive means exist to achieve the legitimate purpose. The assessment identified three fundamental open questions:

| Open Item | Question Under Review | Assessment Status |
|---|---|---|
| `OPEN-SLCD-7` | **Periodic sampling vs continuous observation**: Is continuous 1-2s polling necessary, or would periodic sampling satisfy security attribution? | **UNRESOLVED** — Continuous sampling directly reconstructs employee working hours. |
| `OPEN-SLCD-8` | **Necessity of work-rate metrics (`metrics.*`)**: Are `step_count`, `tokens_in`, `tokens_out`, and `tool_counts` necessary for security attribution, or do they serve only work-rate tracking? | **UNRESOLVED** — Attribution is fully satisfied by `harness`, `session_id`, and `kanban.task_id` alone without numeric work-rate fields. |
| `OPEN-SLCD-9` | **Necessity of transcript parsing**: Can `session_id` and harness identity be derived from the process tree and cgroups without reading on-disk transcript files? | **UNRESOLVED** — Transcript reads introduce proximity risk to customer payloads. |

### 2.3 Limb 3: The Balancing Test — **DOES NOT BALANCE (OPEN)**
The balancing test weighs the controller's interest against the rights and fundamental freedoms of the employee (Article 8 of the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights).

- **Where it Balances**: Basic agent discovery (attributing an active Claude or Codex session to an authorized Kanban task) with zero payload collection balances in favor of the controller.
- **Where it DOES NOT Balance**: Collecting continuous work-rate counters (`tokens_in`, `step_count`, tool invocation rates) on an individual developer's machine creates high surveillance pressure and directly reconstructs working rhythms, failing the balancing test.

## 3. Summary of the 40 OPEN Governance Items

The Phase 1-5 assessment established a rigorous registry of **40 OPEN items**, of which **22 are hard blockers** preventing deployment on employee workstations:

### 3.1 Key Blocking Open Items (Selection)
- **`OPEN-SLCD-1` (In-Scope Workstation Roster)**: No formal roster of in-scope machines exists; without a bounded scope, lawful basis cannot be applied.
- **`OPEN-SLCD-2` (Technical Enforcement of Disabled State)**: The sensor must provide a cryptographic kill-switch ensuring collection cannot be activated remotely without local operator assent.
- **`OPEN-SLCD-4` (Productivity Inference Prevention)**: Technical controls preventing dashboard or API consumers from running productivity aggregations on individual developers.
- **`OPEN-SLCD-7` (Sampling Interval Assessment)**: Formal evaluation of periodic vs continuous observation.
- **`OPEN-SLCD-8` (Removal or Justification of `metrics.*`)**: Formal determination whether work-rate fields must be removed entirely from the schema.
- **`OPEN-SLCD-12` (German Works Council Co-Determination Agreement)**: Mandatory Betriebsrat formal agreement.
- **`OPEN-SLCD-13` (French CSE Consultation)**: Mandatory Comité Social et Économique consultation.
- **`OPEN-SLCD-22` (Data Protection Impact Assessment — DPIA)**: Full formal DPIA signed by Data Protection Officer (DPO).

## 4. Multi-Jurisdictional Works Council Position

| Jurisdiction | Legal Basis / Authority | Co-Determination & Consultation Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Germany** | BetrVG §87(1) Nr. 6 (Technical systems designed to monitor employee behavior/performance) | **Mandatory Co-Determination Required.** Collection without a formal Betriebsvereinbarung (Works Agreement) is legally void and prohibited by federal labor law. |
| **France** | French Labor Code (Code du travail) Art. L2312-38 | **Mandatory CSE Consultation Required.** Prior information and consultation with the Social and Economic Committee (CSE) before deployment. |

| **United States** | State Privacy Laws (e.g. California CPRA §1798.100 et seq., Colorado CPA) | **Employee Notice Required.** Requires notice at collection disclosing categories of telemetry processed. |

## 5. Absolute Prohibition on Developer Productivity Inference

Signal Loom governance policies formally prohibit:
1. **Ranking or Scoring Developers**: Using token counts, tool execution frequencies, or step durations to evaluate developer output or speed.
2. **Attendance or Working Hour Reconstruction**: Using telemetry timestamps or Kanban duration deltas to measure employee working hours or active desk time.
3. **Automated Performance Decisions**: Feeding detective telemetry into HR, promotion, or performance management systems.

Any attempt to configure or query Signal Loom for productivity tracking represents a direct breach of system governance and data protection policy.
